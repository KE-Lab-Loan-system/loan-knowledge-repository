# Frequently Asked Questions

Click a question to see the answer.

<details>
<summary><b>Why was my loan rejected?</b></summary>

Common reasons are a low credit score, too many existing EMIs (high FOIR), unstable income, incomplete documents, or past defaults. The bank should tell you the main reason in writing.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Can I get a loan with a low credit score?</b></summary>

It is harder. You may still get a **secured** loan (e.g. gold or vehicle loan) or apply with a co-applicant who has a good score, often at a higher interest rate.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>I have never taken a loan. Will I be rejected?</b></summary>

Not necessarily. You have "no credit history" (NH). Banks do extra checks on income and job stability. A small loan or credit card repaid on time helps build history.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>How much loan can I get?</b></summary>

It depends mainly on your income and existing EMIs. Most banks keep total EMIs within about 50–60% of monthly income.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>What is the difference between secured and unsecured loans?</b></summary>

A secured loan is backed by an asset (house, car, gold). An unsecured loan (e.g. personal loan) is not, so it usually needs a better credit score and has a higher interest rate.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Does applying at many banks hurt my score?</b></summary>

Many loan applications in a short time can lower your score, because each one creates a hard enquiry.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Why do education loans need a co-applicant?</b></summary>

The student usually has no income yet, so a parent or guardian shares the responsibility for repayment.

</details>
